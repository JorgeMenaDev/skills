# Commercial Integrity

Load for comparisons, alternatives, rankings, listicles, community-source pages, affiliate/referral pages, or any inclusion/order influenced by ownership or material benefit. Use the shared evidence terms in `references/evidence-conventions.md`.

## Selection and comparison contract

- Publish a reproducible selection methodology: purpose, audience, eligible set, inclusion/exclusion criteria, evidence sources, checked date, evaluation method, and limitations. Explain ordering and re-run the method when material facts change.
- Disclose ownership and self-inclusion wherever the publisher, product, client, or related entity appears. Never imply independent evaluation when the publisher has an interest.
- Disclose any payment, commission, gifted access, sponsorship, referral benefit, or other material benefit that could influence inclusion, ordering, or recommendation.
- Put the disclosure next to the affected claim, recommendation, CTA, code, or link—not only in a footer or generic policy. It must remain visible without expansion on a typical mobile viewport and survive syndication or format changes.
- Link directly to each named alternative's relevant official page so readers can verify the comparison. Do not route through an affiliate redirect when a direct evidence link is needed.
- Show a visible “last checked” or update date on the page and date material comparison evidence. Recheck rather than silently carrying stale claims forward.

Substantiation belongs to `references/page-evidence.md` when that reference lands. Sponsored-link attributes and backlink mechanics belong to `references/backlinks-entity.md`; conversion-event definitions belong to `references/conversion-cta.md`.

## Anti-authority-rental boundary

Third-party publishing must serve that host's audience with original, useful content and transparent relationships. Do not publish primarily to exploit the host domain's authority, manufacture independence, pass ranking signals, or feed opaque indexers. Reject parasite publishing, rented subfolders/subdomains, paid placement disguised as editorial inclusion, and pages whose value disappears without the host's authority.
