# Local SEO And Google Business Profile

Use only when the business has local intent, service areas, a storefront, GBP, map-pack competitors, reviews, or citations.

## Operating Pattern

1. Load business context: NAP, GBP URL, services, service areas, competitors, top keywords.
2. Use Google Maps/GBP/public listings or authenticated GBP where available.
3. Compare the business against 3-5 competitors.
4. Output a matrix, then prioritized actions with impact and time-to-result.
5. Write the actual descriptions, review responses, post calendar, or citation fixes where requested.

## Modules

| Module           | Evidence                                                                           | Output                                             |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| GBP categories   | Map-pack searches for target service/city, competitor primary/secondary categories | Missing category priority list                     |
| GBP attributes   | Visible listing attributes/tags                                                    | Table-stakes vs differentiation attributes         |
| Reviews          | Review count/rating/velocity, last 30/60/90 days, service/location phrases         | Review velocity target and review request language |
| Customer language | Reviews, calls, support notes, testimonials, social comments, competitor reviews    | Copy implications for site, GBP, CTAs, and scripts |
| Review responses | Response rate, tone, service/city mentions, negative review handling               | Response template set by star rating               |
| GBP posts        | Post frequency, type, CTA, image usage, topic/location mentions                    | 4-8 week post calendar with copy/outlines          |
| Services         | Listed services/descriptions vs site and competitors                               | Optimized service descriptions                     |
| Description      | GBP descriptions and competitor patterns                                           | 3 description variants under platform limits       |
| Photos           | Photo count, recency, types, quality, local signals                                | Weekly upload plan and shot list                   |
| Citations        | NAP across directories and duplicates                                              | Fix list and missing directory list                |
| Local intent     | Keyword stages from problem to ready-to-hire                                       | Keyword-to-page/GBP strategy                       |

## Matrix Columns

Use columns such as:

`competitor`, `rank`, `category`, `attribute`, `review count`, `review velocity`, `service`, `city`, `evidence URL`, `gap`, `impact`, `time-to-result`, `next action`.

## Customer-Language Mining

Use this when copy sounds generic or when the business has enough reviews/testimonials to extract real buyer language. Compare the target against competitors when competitor reviews are public.

Capture:

- Top fears or frustrations mentioned before purchase/service.
- Top outcomes customers celebrate.
- Emotional words customers use.
- Recommendation phrases customers use when telling others.
- Language patterns in 5-star reviews that are missing from 3-star reviews.
- Copy implications for homepage, service pages, GBP description, review request scripts, social proof, and CTAs.

Do not invent customer quotes. Paraphrase patterns unless the source allows quoting and the quote is short, accurate, and attributed.

## GBP Posting Pattern

When GBP posts matter, compare recent posts for the target and competitors with one row per post:

`post date/time`, `day of week`, `post type`, `word count`, `CTA button`, `image used`, `topic/service`, `neighborhood/city`, `price/offer`, `seasonal pattern`, `competitor cadence gap`.

Output a cadence recommendation and the first 4 weeks of posts when relevant. Keep post copy grounded in real services, real locations, and real offers.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent addresses, phone numbers, certifications, service areas, reviews, or photos.
- Do not recommend fake review language. Suggest prompts that help happy customers mention real service/location details.
- If authenticated GBP changes are possible, record before/after evidence and avoid changing legal business details without explicit confirmation.
